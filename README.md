# Bayesian ReLU regression: simulation and reproducibility materials

Simulation code and numerical results accompanying the article by Hien Duy Nguyen, Jacob Westerhout and Julyan Arbel.

The study uses a fractional posterior with likelihood power `eta = 0.9` and 50 independent data replications for each target, parameter family and sample size. The sample sizes are `64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096`. All 2,100 fitted parameter cases satisfy the stated diagnostic requirements. Gaussian width comparisons share posterior samples, giving 3,500 integrated squared errors and 70 complete comparison cells.

## Statistical setting

The regression targets on `[0,1]` are `sqrt(abs(sin(2*pi*x)))`, with Hölder exponent `1/2`, and `abs(sin(2*pi*x))`, which is Lipschitz. Covariates are uniform on `[0,1]`; errors are independent standard normal variables. The prior is a sum of ReLU components with random width. Numerical calculations restrict the width to at most 64.

The five comparisons are:

| Label | Network parameters | Width distribution |
|---|---|---|
| N/G | Gaussian | Geometric, mass proportional to `2^-K` |
| N/P | Gaussian | Shifted Poisson |
| N/H | Gaussian | Power law |
| U/G | Uniform | Geometric |
| t10/G | Student `t10` | Geometric |

The N/P and N/H estimates use importance weights applied to the N/G posterior samples. Uniform hidden coordinates lie in `[-2,2]`, and uniform output coefficients lie in `[-K^2,K^2]`. Gaussian output variance is `K^3`; Student parameters follow unit-scale `t10` distributions. Complete prior and computational specifications are in the article and `protocol.json`.

## Contents

- `Refit.R` reproduces the reported fitted configurations or the prescribed sequence of computation stages.
- `Replot.R` checks numerical results and reconstructs figure and table values without fitting any model.
- `source/` contains the R and C++ implementations.
- `records/selected_native_fits.csv` gives the 2,100 fitted cases, replication indices, data and chain seeds, settings and diagnostics.
- `records/selected_risks.csv` gives the 3,500 integrated squared errors.
- `records/risk_cells.csv` gives means, Monte Carlo standard errors and pointwise 95% normal confidence intervals for the 70 comparison cells.
- `records/rate_intervals.csv` gives the slope summaries and paired bootstrap intervals.
- The credible-interval records give posterior means and pointwise interval endpoints for the four uniform and four Gaussian examples.
- `figures/` contains the study figures and numerical table values.
- `figures/supplementary_half_table.tex` and `figures/supplementary_lipschitz_table.tex` give the supplementary tables of means, standard errors and confidence intervals, regenerated by `Replot.R` from the numerical results.
- `paper/main_arxiv.tex` contains the article, appendices and integrated references; `paper/main_arxiv.pdf` is the compiled article.
- `protocol.json` specifies the diagnostic thresholds, seed formulas, eight original computation stages and later amendment for the single unresolved fit.
- `runtime_versions.json` describes the numerical environment used for the published calculations.
- `SOURCE_PROVENANCE.json` gives checksums of the unchanged simulation materials from the verified final study and identifies the later table presentation changes.

The identifiers `replicate` and `original_replication_id` refer to replications 1–50. For each target, the same indexed data stream is used across parameter families and sample sizes, with nested observations as `n` increases. The seed indexing is intentionally retained: sample sizes 4096 and 2048 use indices 6 and 8, respectively.

## Check the results and reproduce the figures

From this directory, with R installed:

```sh
Rscript Replot.R unused --check-only
Rscript Refit.R unused --check-only
```

These commands validate the complete numerical results and fitted configurations without running Markov chains or writing figures. To reconstruct the figures and numerical summaries in a new directory:

```sh
Rscript Replot.R reconstructed
```

Figure production also requires Python 3 with `pypdf`. Optional PNG rendering uses Poppler's `pdftoppm`. The `PYTHON` and `PDFTOPPM` environment variables select alternative executable locations.

For each replication, integrated squared error is approximated by the equally weighted mean squared error of the clipped posterior mean on the study's fixed 1009-point shifted grid. Cell means average 50 errors. Their Monte Carlo standard errors are `sd(error)/sqrt(50)`, and normal intervals are `mean +/- 1.96*SE`. Slope intervals use 2,000 percentile-bootstrap resamples of replication indices, paired across sample sizes. The same resamples are used across comparisons within a target; their seeds are 20261507 and 20262007 for the Hölder and Lipschitz targets, respectively. These fixed-target summaries do not establish worst-case contraction rates or bound residual Markov-chain error.

Uniform credible intervals use empirical quantiles of the clipped function draws, with R quantile type 1. Gaussian credible intervals invert the equally weighted conditional Gaussian mixture distribution after clipping. Both are 95% pointwise intervals for the latent regression function; observation noise is not added.

## Refit the reported configurations

Fitting the models is computationally expensive. Install the R packages `Rcpp`, `RcppArmadillo` and `coda`, together with a C++ compiler configured for R. To reproduce a single fitted case:

```sh
Rscript Refit.R refitted --rows=1 --workers=1
```

To reproduce all reported configurations:

```sh
Rscript Refit.R refitted --workers=4
```

Each row supplies its exact data seed, chain seeds, selected stage, iteration count, number of independent chains, half burn-in, thinning, likelihood-power ladder, width limit and clipping bound. The command reruns that configuration and compares errors and diagnostics with the published values. A comma-separated `--rows` argument selects several cases. Windows uses sequential execution.

To start at stage 1 for every selected case and advance only after diagnostic failure:

```sh
Rscript Refit.R full_study --all-stages --workers=10
```

The original eight stages use `24000, 48000, 96000, 192000, 384000, 768000, 1536000, 3072000` iterations with `3, 4, 6, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8` chains. The number of concurrent fits is limited to `10, 10, 10, 8, 4, 2, 1, 1`, subject to the requested worker limit. A configuration is selected at its first passing stage; squared error and credible coverage do not influence selection.

After the uniform-parameter fit for the Hölder target at `n=4096`, replication 15, exhausted those eight stages, four additional stages were authorized for that same dataset and fit. Their iteration budgets are `6144000, 12288000, 24576000, 49152000`, with eight chains, half burn-in, thinning 20 and one concurrent fit. This later amendment is explicit in `protocol.json`; the original plan, dataset, prior, sampler and diagnostic thresholds are preserved. Only this fit is eligible for stages 9–12, and its first passing stage is 10. An unsuccessful final stage stops the calculation rather than adding stages or replacing the replication.

For exact reproduction, data are generated in the original order: 1024 covariates followed by 1024 errors, followed when needed by 3072 more covariates and 3072 more errors; the required prefix is then taken. The Hölder target uses the exact expression given above. All chains discard the first half of iterations and retain every twentieth subsequent iteration. For the four uniform credible-interval examples, additional function samples are obtained using the same seeds and transitions; all original numerical outputs and the final random-number-generator state are checked for exact agreement.

The diagnostic requirements are Rhat at most 1.10 and effective sample size at least 50 for width and the five function probes, with the corresponding clipped probes checked as well. No post-burn-in iteration may reach width 64. These checks do not guarantee exploration of every posterior mode or bound finite-chain error. Small numerical differences can depend on R, compiler, BLAS and package versions. Refits save their environment information and stop if the required comparisons fail.
